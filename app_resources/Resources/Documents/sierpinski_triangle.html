Sierpinski Triangle

A triangle made mostly of absence.

This fractal is built by repetition so simple it feels like a magic trick: remove the middle triangle, then do the same to every remaining triangle forever.

What to try first

Look at the largest empty triangle, then find the same emptiness repeated inside each corner.

Zoom into any corner. The image should feel unchanged because each part contains smaller copies of the whole.

Use this page as a calm interlude after chaotic escape-time fractals. Here the rule is geometric rather than turbulent.

Mathematical notes

Dimension between line and surface

The Sierpinski triangle contains three half-size copies of itself. Its similarity dimension is therefore \(\log(3)/\log(2)\), about \(1.585\). That is larger than a line but smaller than a filled area, matching the way it spreads across the plane while leaving holes everywhere.

The chaos game version

A common construction starts from any point, repeatedly chooses one triangle vertex at random, and moves halfway toward it. After many steps the points reveal the Sierpinski triangle. Random choices create a deterministic shape because the transformations contract space toward the same invariant set.
