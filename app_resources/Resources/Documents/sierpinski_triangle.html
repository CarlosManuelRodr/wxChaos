Sierpinski Triangle

A self-similar triangle rendered as a complex-plane membership test.

Formula

The classical set can be made by repeatedly removing the middle triangle. wxChaos instead uses an inverse-map trick on the complex plane: starting from \(z=(x,y)\), it doubles the point and subtracts one unit from the coordinate that lies in the upper or right half-cell. Points whose iterates avoid escape remain in the triangle.

wxChaos renderer trick

The renderer divides the unit square into three affine branches: top, right, and lower-left. Each branch maps a small copy of the triangle back onto the full triangle. Iterating this reverse map is similar in spirit to an escape-time fractal: points in removed gaps eventually fly away, while points in the invariant gasket keep landing on valid copies.

Background

Waclaw Sierpinski described this gasket in the early twentieth century, but the pattern also appears in Pascal's triangle modulo 2, cellular automata, and the chaos game. Its charm is that a simple rule creates detail at every scale.

wxChaos capabilities

  • High precision rendering.
  • Custom non-escape-time renderer based on affine inverse branches.
